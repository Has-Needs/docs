# Society begins with people. Technology should, too.
### Has-Needs: a sovereign protocol for how we help one another, work together, and build trust.

**What do you have? What do you need? What can we accomplish together?**

Every person has needs, knowledge, abilities, relationships, and experience. Those things make society work. But our digital lives are scattered across services that hold our identities, store our interactions, and decide which histories we can carry elsewhere.

**Has-Needs turns the arrangement around.** Instead of placing everyone inside a platform, it builds a protected means of participation around each individual. You can seek help, offer what you have, join a community, make agreements, and retain evidence of what happened—without allowing a company, agency, or network to own your social existence.

This is not just a new way to find resources. It is a different foundation for participating in society.

## The person is the starting point—and the destination

Imagine taking your experience and relationships with you wherever you go. You could show that you've delivered supplies, repaired equipment, assisted in an emergency, or contributed to a community effort, without exposing everything else about your life.

Has-Needs is designed to give individuals that kind of portable continuity. You hold your own record of completed interactions. Claims about your abilities can be supported by attestations from communities and people who know your work. Matching records of exchanges add another form of evidence. You decide which details are relevant to each new situation.

Your needs and offers travel outward into places where they may be useful. Responses return to you, for **you** to consider. Agreements are made on terms you accept. Outcomes return to your history, strengthening what you know and can demonstrate.

The network carries information. **The person retains the agency, the relationships, and the record.**

## Two simple ways to begin

**I NEED…** a ride, an answer, a repair, emergency supplies, a research collaborator, or help with a larger goal.

**I HAVE…** a vehicle, a skill, a tool, local knowledge, available time, or the willingness to participate.

Has-Needs helps these intentions find one another. Geographic relevance and community context can make a match useful without requiring anyone to broadcast their actual location. A person can present different facets of themselves as a neighbor, worker, parent, or volunteer, without turning them into one publicly searchable profile.

A Need is more than a posting: it can describe what satisfactory help would look like, and the terms required before action begins.

## Discovery is not a decision

Here is the entire interaction in human terms:

1. **Express.** Someone describes a Need or a Has, with only the information they choose to make available.
2. **Discover.** Software—including AI where useful—offers compatible possibilities based on permitted context, proximity, and experience.
3. **Choose.** The people involved inspect the relevant information, decide whether they want to proceed, and agree on terms. The AI does not accept for them.
4. **Work.** Their accepted relationship becomes **Working**. The commitment and agreed progress are recorded as pending, so a promise is not mistaken for an accomplishment.
5. **Resolve.** An authorized outcome is documented in matching receipts held in each participant's personal history. A cancellation, partial result, or unresolved dispute is not dressed up as successful fulfillment.

There is no requirement for a central inventory of every person and everything they need. Discovery happens within appropriate, permissioned scopes, not through a universal list of private lives.

## One blocked road. A clearer picture for everyone.

Flooding has cut off a neighborhood. Someone reports that a road needs clearing. A local operator has an excavator. Another resident knows which route is passable.

Has-Needs can bring those possibilities together. The people involved choose what they can do and make an agreement. The job now appears as committed work—not as a completed road repair. Authorized community members and responders can see what still needs attention without having to take custody of everyone's private information.

When the road is cleared, the participants attest to the outcome and retain matching receipts. That record helps them demonstrate what they accomplished and may help future matching recognize what worked in similar circumstances.

The resident was never merely a data point in a distant dashboard. The request originated with a person, possible responses came back to that person, and the outcome became part of the participants' own histories.

The same relationship can continue after the flood. The capability to participate doesn't disappear when an emergency program closes.

## A social system designed to reduce harm and friction

Has-Needs began with a humanitarian challenge: disasters can strip people of control precisely when being able to speak, choose, and act matters most. Systems that treat affected people only as reports to be collected can reinforce that loss of agency.

**The design goal is trauma mitigation through meaningful participation.** Affected people can express needs directly, decide what to share, work with others, follow accepted commitments, and keep evidence of outcomes. This is a design aim, not a claim that software alone can prevent or clinically treat trauma.

And the problem is larger than disaster. Society generates friction whenever finding the right person, communicating a need, agreeing on a response, and knowing what happened require separate institutions, accounts, or gatekeepers. Has-Needs applies one understandable interaction pattern across those settings.

## Your history can speak without exposing your identity

A person's record can contain offers, accepted commitments, receipts of resolved exchanges, and claims supported by relevant attestations. The system is intended to distinguish what someone declares from what others attest to, and from what completed transactions actually establish.

That evidence can inform human judgment. Someone considering a new exchange might inspect a permitted history of prior work or common connections instead of relying on a public star rating or an opaque trust score.

Has-Needs also separates *where a need can be met* from *where a person is*. It aims to support relevant matching without publishing exact personal locations. Disclosures are bounded by the relationship and chosen purpose.

Uniqueness and continuity can be attested at the level an interaction requires, without routinely demanding civil identity. More sensitive exchanges can call for stronger evidence when necessary.

## Communities form through purpose, not platform ownership

Five neighbors can pool tools. Five hundred residents can coordinate after an earthquake. Five thousand people across a region can contribute knowledge, transport, food, or specialized equipment.

People can establish communities around shared needs, choose how to participate, and authorize specific kinds of pooled action. Larger groups can form smaller groups when the work calls for it. Nobody has to give a community blanket control over their personal resources merely by joining.

An emergency agency, research team, cooperative, or municipality can take part on the same terms of consent and accountable interaction. They can receive authorized views of needs and progress without taking over people's individual histories.

This makes cooperation more than a temporary feature of an application. It becomes an enduring ability people bring into new settings.

## Three useful functions from one interaction

Finding help, coordinating action, and documenting results are usually separate jobs handled by separate systems.

Has-Needs brings them together using a small shared grammar: **an entity, a relationship, and its context**.

That simple form supports:

- **Discovery:** finding relevant people, resources, and possible paths to a goal.
- **Coordination:** making agreements and seeing which commitments are accepted, underway, or unresolved.
- **Evidence and learning:** retaining matching receipts of resolved outcomes and using permitted, real-world experience to improve future suggestions.

Crucially, a proposed match is not a human choice, an accepted commitment is not a completed result, and a cryptographic receipt is not automatic proof that every physical claim was true. The distinctions are what make the information useful.

Knowledge gained through completed exchanges, including Indigenous wisdom and traditional knowledge where its holders choose to share it, remains under the authority of the people and communities who hold it. Has-Needs does not turn it into an outside-owned inventory or globally searchable dataset.

## Useful on an ordinary Tuesday. Essential when circumstances change.

**Everyday life:** A neighbor finds a skill nearby. A group organizes a shared project. A farmer finds transport for a harvest. A small business locates a collaborator.

**Science and learning:** Researchers publish needs for willing participants or expertise. Individuals decide whether to take part and what information to share, under explicit terms.

**Governance:** A resident expresses a need for a service. If the responsible office accepts a commitment, the agreed work can be tracked. Authorized observers can distinguish promises, delays, and completed outcomes.

**Disaster response:** People express first-hand needs and capabilities, responders see the work they've been authorized to see, and the community remains an active source of decisions—not simply reports.

The underlying social ability is the same in every case. That's why daily use matters: people shouldn't have to learn a new model of society in the middle of a crisis.

## The network is a means, not the owner

The architecture is designed for different ways of communicating: internet connections, nearby devices, local relays, low-bandwidth messages, radio, and even human-assisted transfer where appropriate.

If rich services fail, the essential process remains intelligible: someone needs something, another person can help, they choose to work together, and they preserve what happened.

Has-Needs is being specified and evaluated as a protocol; individual transport and security mechanisms require implementation and testing. Its promise is an architectural one: **your ability to participate should not belong to the network you happen to be using.**

## What becomes possible?

A society in which a person's voice can travel outward and meaningful responses come back. Where capability can be recognized without compulsory exposure. Where communities form by consent. Where accepted obligations stay visible and actual results become shared evidence. Where the same human-centered structure supports everyday life and the extraordinary demands of disaster.

**A person should not have to surrender their social identity to participate in society.**

Has-Needs proposes infrastructure built around that principle.

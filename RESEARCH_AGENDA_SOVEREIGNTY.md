# Has-Needs: Interdisciplinary Research Questions and Composable Sovereignty

*Companion to RESEARCH_WHITE_PAPER_COMPANION.md. Research hypotheses, not additional V1 protocol rules or claims of established legal rights.*

## Research questions across disciplines

**Psychology and trauma.** Does practical agency—expressing a Need, choosing a counterparty, and authorizing disclosure—improve perceived control, dignity, self-efficacy, or recovery, even when material assistance is unchanged?

**Sociology and economics.** How much failure of cooperation results from transaction and coordination costs rather than unwillingness to cooperate? Does lowering the cost of forming voluntary communities increase collective action without demanding altruism?

**Complexity science.** Can the same Has/Need/Working grammar coordinate people, communities, and institutions at increasing scales without proportionate bureaucratic growth? Where do delegation and distributed governance fail?

**Disaster resilience.** When does scarcity promote pooling and reciprocal cooperation rather than competition, and which participants are excluded?

**Information economics.** What is the cost and accuracy of directly expressed, actionable intent compared with needs inferred through surveys, surveillance, outreach, and analytics? Which expenditures can actually be avoided?

**Public administration.** Can accountability emerge from ordinary accepted commitments, response times, and outcomes rather than separate reporting systems? Can visibility perversely incentivize institutions to avoid difficult commitments?

**Human–AI interaction.** Can AI discover useful equivalences from human-approved exchanges without deciding human value, consent, or outcomes? What happens to minority preferences and underrepresented communities?

## Sovereign identity and human provenance

As synthetic voices, images, documents, and agents become easier to generate, can people establish human uniqueness and authorization without exposing comprehensive identity records? Distinguish proof of a unique participant, proof that a particular person authorized an act, and proof that a claim is true. None automatically implies the others.

Can individual control of identity facets reduce institutional classification power without creating new gatekeepers in credential issuance? Can a verifiable record of an authorized interaction become more useful than a convincing representation of a person's face or voice?

## Reversal of the data authorization burden

**Design hypothesis:** valid use of personal information is accompanied by a verifiable, purpose-bounded authorization token. An absent, expired, revoked, or mismatched token is a protocol-level authorization failure that can trigger denial of access, quarantine, notice, or review. A protocol violation does not automatically constitute unlawful possession or use under existing law. Legal enforceability depends on applicable legislation, contracts, and remedies.

Research questions: Can requiring custodians to demonstrate authorization shift enforcement costs away from individuals? What legal reforms would make absence of valid authorization actionable? Can authorization checks be performed without disclosing the personal information they protect? What exceptions, due-process safeguards, and competing rights are necessary?

## Composable sovereignty: authorization, API access, and revocation

The architecture connects three complementary functions:

1. **Contract token:** verifiable evidence identifying the permitted recipient, purpose, scope, duration, and applicable conditions.
2. **Composable API:** a controlled interface exposing only the authorized fields, computations, or data streams for each relationship. Permissions may be assembled differently for a healthcare provider, research group, civic institution, or commercial buyer without handing each unrestricted access to the underlying store.
3. **Revocation:** the participant can terminate future access through the controlled channel, subject to the contract and applicable law. This makes authority continuing rather than exhausted at initial disclosure.

This arrangement proposes a shift from transferring unrestricted copies toward licensing bounded capabilities. Revoking an API cannot retract copies already exported, erase model training, or eliminate statutory retention duties. Research must test whether contractual obligations, auditing, and technical safeguards can constrain those downstream uses.

**Core question:** Can composable, purpose-limited, revocable access let people participate in civic and commercial exchanges while retaining materially greater control over their information?

Measure unauthorized access attempts, successful revocations, exposure of unnecessary fields, bargaining outcomes, participant comprehension, administrative overhead, and violations after data export.

## Economic participation and global evidentiary equality

An individual may offer a Has representing a creative work, data product, or authorized data stream. Another participant expresses a Need; both can negotiate use, payment, and duration. The participant remains a counterparty with continuing rights and responsibilities, rather than merely a passive data source.

Could this redistribute economic value from data intermediaries to individuals? Does equality of technical capability lead to equality of bargaining power or actual income? Could cryptographically verifiable provenance and licensing give creators in less-resourced jurisdictions access to comparable evidentiary tools?

Cryptographic integrity, timestamps, and signatures may support evidence of a record and key control, but do not alone prove original authorship, copyright ownership, legal admissibility, or enforceability across borders. Compare these separately.

## The rocks, sand, and water analogy

A teacher fills a jar with rocks and asks whether it is full. Sand then fits between the rocks; water subsequently fills spaces between the grains. **Banks and other established institutions are the rocks; people are the sand; Has-Needs is the water.**

The point is not that existing structures must be displaced. A shared, sovereignty-preserving coordination layer can operate among people and institutions as they already exist, making relationships possible that previously cost too much to discover, authorize, or fulfill. Composable APIs allow participation without surrendering entire data stores; verifiable contracts and revocation preserve authority within those relationships.

**Testable question:** Does adding an interoperable coordination layer increase useful exchanges and productive activity within an existing social structure without requiring central reorganization? Compare discovered matches, completed exchanges, coordination costs, participation, and institutional workload with appropriate controls.

## Integrating hypothesis

To what extent are limitations attributed to human nature consequences of coordination, information, identity, and authority structures? Has-Needs offers one test environment. Evaluate favorable, null, and adverse results, including exclusion, coercion, concentration of power, and failures of revocation.

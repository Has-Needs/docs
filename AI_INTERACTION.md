# Has-Needs and AI: learning the inversion

This companion page preserves the interpretive lessons that emerged as AI assistants learned to understand Has-Needs. It is a **teaching and design resource**, not an amendment to the [canonical V1 specification](./Has-Needs-Spec-v1.md).

> Has-Needs is not a system for describing resources. It is a system for discovering and preserving successful state transitions.
>
> The ontology is not the truth; it is the current map of what has worked.

Those two propositions explain why familiar labels can mislead. Systems built around provider-owned accounts, predefined categories, central registries, synthetic trust scores, or extracted behavioral data may resemble an individual Has-Needs component while reversing the architecture's locus of authority.

## Learn from the misunderstandings

The useful historical record is the **delta between the assistant's conventional starting assumption and the founder's correction**. It is retained intentionally, separate from the specification, so the V1 architecture can state affirmative principles without repeating every past debate.

- **[The Paradigm Delta](./Has-Needs_Paradigm_Delta_Review_2026-10-14.md)** — longitudinal comparisons, misconception → correction → conceptual teaching, with dated revision references.
- **[Assistant Orientation](./Has-Needs_Assistant_Orientation_2026-10-14.md)** — short interpretive guide for assistants explaining the project or helping develop it.
- **[One-page introduction](./INTRODUCTION.md)** — the human-facing explanation to offer first.
- **[Specification V1](./Has-Needs-Spec-v1.md)** — authoritative architecture and protocol requirements.

An assistant should read from the sovereign participant outward: the individual originates meaning, chooses relationships and disclosures, and retains reciprocal evidence. A matching suggestion is not a commitment, a recorded commitment is not completion, and cryptographic integrity is not proof of every physical-world claim. Services and AI can assist within granted scope; they do not inherit the person's authority.

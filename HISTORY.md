# Has-Needs — origins and development history

A personal account of the experiences, ideas, and work behind Has-Needs.

## Dedication to Ishi

> This work is dedicated to the memory and legacy of Ishi, whose story as the last known member of his people echoed through my own foster-childhood, teaching me resilience and perspective. In Ishi’s quiet courage and unbroken dignity, I learned the value of personal sovereignty and how mutual respect knits impossible divides.

## Childhood, movement, and continuity

I entered foster care in seventh grade. In an advanced English class, a teacher brought river stones from Ishi's home region and invited us to choose one while reading his story. Nature was already my greatest source of refuge, hope, and strength, and I felt an affinity with the displacement and isolation in that story. That stone, like most of my treasured belongings, disappeared during childhood moves and was lost to time.

I moved more than fifty times before age twenty. Those years included stays in a castle in Italy, a cottage in Grasse, and a château in Antibes, followed by hardship in the USA, food scavenging, and ultimately foster care. Each relocation brought a different world of relationships, dangers, opportunities, and local knowledge. Many of my own decisions might have been different if more of the surrounding situation had been visible to me. Additionally, gaps in memory associated with trauma interfered with preserving meaningful history without artifacts and treasures. The result was my fractured timeline with no real anchor to community.

These experiences shaped my concern for the **self-quantified sovereign individual**: retaining continuity of identity, relationships, contributions, and commitments whether rooted or mobile.

## Agency, trauma, and systems thinking

My early exposure to Buckminster Fuller, peer counseling since High School in 1985, emergency and mass-casualty response as a paramedic, post-trauma debriefing, and participation in a UCSF stress/trauma study - informs my thinking about agency and resilience.

My continuing design goal is trauma mitigation through practical empowerment: giving people direct ways to speak, choose, contribute, and cooperate. **Sovereign Survivor +1** expresses this starting point. A survivor is a sovereign actor, who is able to connect with at least one other sovereign participant, to enact a change. My life had also shown that people affected by a disaster have knowledge of their circumstances also need timely information to make sense of their present and to heal.

## Early activism and community sovereignty

Environmental and social-justice organizing preceded the software. I had been involved in activism since childhood. My first independent campaign was at **UCSB**, concerning university divestment in apartheid-era South Africa, and support for **Nelson Mandela**. I continued participating in direct action and social movements, including the **WTO protests in Seattle** and **Melbourne, Australia**.

Around **2000**, I worked directly with a family group among the **Dja Dja Wurrung (Jaara) Traditional Owners** of the Bendigo region in Victoria, Australia. A group without ancestral affiliation to the local clan was administering government funding through the area's community centre. I supported a rematriation of the community centre, then undertook forensic computer analysis of the administrative records and identified financial misuse benefiting a small group of associates. My findings helped the Dja Dja Wurrung family group gain permanent control of the community centre.

That return of local control enabled culturally appropriate administration and community services, with work toward rematriation of cultural artifacts, cultural education, and health-related facilities on their Country. It was a practical experience of community sovereignty: people gaining control of the resources intended for them.

The eventual **2013 Dja Dja Wurrung Recognition and Settlement Agreement** with the State of Victoria was a landmark event in formal recognition of Traditional Owner rights.

## Global networks and local relevance

My early membership in Nomads International, and emerging electronic bulletin board systems (BBS), and later 2600-oriented network culture brought excitement at the collapse of distance the internet would provide. I shared in the collective dismay when the W3C essentially gave sovereign identity away to commercial platforms, who subsequently enclosed and partitioned social groups, creating the extractive data economy that affects us all today.

My attention turned toward seeing the world immediately around oneself differently. In a disaster, when possessions and institutions are gone, a person still occupies physical space. I began asking: *Who does location matter to, and when? Where do personal/institutional interests intersect, and how can those intersections be supported outside of the commercial venue?*

## 1996–97: Environmental engagement and the satellite globe idea

The starting idea for globe based data was a **land-sailing app that made environmental education feel like play**. I was developing a handheld wingsail and protective pads, and envisioned draping satellite data on a globe to show environmental conditions. I reasoned that wind, geography, and air quality would directly affect where people could go and how much fun they would have. Airborne particulates could spoil an outing, making environmental conditions personally relevant, and hopefully stimulating a response into action.

I sought out a NASA aeronautics researcher, found **Ilan Kroo**, and discussed ways to connect satellite imagery to an interactive globe for regular consumer use.

## November 1999: NASA technology exhibition in Miami Beach

I recall discussing the globe and satellite-data access concept with NASA representatives at **Tech East ’99**, held at the **Fontainebleau Hilton** in Miami Beach. I was told that work on a globe was already underway, much to my delight, it was going to be called World Wind.

##World Wind developer community

NASA World Wind development began in 2002, with the original application publicly released in 2004. The open-source application and its community enabled developers to layer and explore geographic information on a virtual globe and by 2005 it was a thriving group.

My globe+data ideas evolved toward general-purpose geographic visualization and the mapping of human needs. In 2006, **EnterChange** was another incarnation of that broader vision that I tried to spark investor interest with.

I also worked with **Greg Gollberg of U-Idaho** discussing GIS-Globe based fire-prediction tooling.

**In 2007, I commissioned a NASA World Wind plugin** that ingested CSV files and presented georeferenced information on the globe using icons and colors. The intent was to make disparate data sources useful for human-oriented geographic visualization, including expressing people's needs. 

In **November 2007**, the **United Nations Development Programme, Google, and Cisco** introduced the **Millennium Development Goals Monitor**, including a Google Earth layer for geographic development indicators.

This surprised me after the World Wind community discussions. We had hoped that World Wind's open-source tools would be used for efforts of this kind. Access to useful, reliable data remained a broader challenge regardless of the visualization platform.

I submitted **the finished visualization project, its NASA World Wind CSV-to-globe plugin, and a working demonstration to the Imagine Cup**, whose 2009 competition theme addressed the Millennium Development Goals.

## 2009: Emergency-management coursework

In **2009**, I was taking **emergency-management courses at Capella University**, continuing my study of disaster response, coordination, and community resilience before the Haiti earthquake.

## 2010: The Haiti turning point

During the Haiti earthquake response hackathons, I watched the highest level of humanitarian, academic, and technical specialists struggle to obtain timely information about conditions on the ground. I suggested that affected people could call and report their observations and needs. I recall the room laughing and being told that human reports were regarded as untrustworthy across the response system. I later learned how pervasive this philosophy was, spreading through all levels of aid organization and affiliated service providers. It was that moment that galvanized my dedication to making the individual "trustable".

My initial suggestion became a call-in mechanism that received citizen messages, volunteers translated and categorized them, then fed them into the reporting system. My broader proposal for sovereign, reciprocal participation "Has One - Needs One" did not get support at that time.

The distinction between **collecting information from a person** and **empowering that person to participate** became central to my designs. Securing the individual and enabling direct discovery, cooperation, agreements, and reciprocal history became the motivating architecture of Has-Needs.

## 2010–13: UMass Boston

After Haiti, I attended a **post-earthquake forum at the University of Massachusetts Boston**. The people I met and the institution's approach convinced me that targeted academic study could help advance Has-Needs while the technical foundation was still being developed by the broader crypto scene. I pursued a **dual degree in psychology and sociology**, graduating in **2013**.

I deliberately shaped my curriculum around **trauma, Indigenous studies, and epigenetics** to inform the architecture. I wanted to understand how people recover agency, exercise sovereignty, participate in communities, and respond to adversity.

Has-Needs has been a dedicated design effort since the 2010 Haiti response, building on my earlier environmental, geographic, and social-justice work.

## 2013 and Beyond

As a technical writer for IBM middleware, I had an opportunity to present Has-Needs' sovereignty solution to an internal hackathon. My bottom-up disaster management approach was literally opposite of the typical emergency pattern. However, each design choice was built from real world experiences both locally and internationally. I had tried to break it, but Has-Needs' primitives were robust and effective, so I had confidence my system would show well. It was not chosen and the deadline was even extended to allow more entrants. The ultimate winner was called project OWL and the lineage is almost 1:1 directly from my project except for one thing. OWL was designed for emergency operations to be the focus of information flow. Yes it simplified data gathering and dissemination, but being centralized outside of the person on the ground, permanently fails to be as resilient as a fully distributed paradigm.

## Participatory mapping and community knowledge

I understood sovereignty was foundational to trauma reduction. Personal data use had to exist within that, protecting while sharing, and making use of granular access. Beyond the self however, how would recognition and actions be moderated across cultural and linguistic barriers? How would personal concerns become meaningful to a whole system?

The Wave Fax and Map Kibera were formative observations in my life. Wave Fax provided over the horizon perspective and forecasts that made surfing more enjoyable. The cultural shift from literal 'drifters' to informed sport enthusiasts was profound in the surfing community. Map Kibera illustrated how peril can shift to responsiveness in one region just by putting a place on a map. My experience also included participatory action research, experience sampling, citizen science, five intensive years as SBTF crisis mapper, Uchaguzi's Kenya election monitoring, Harvard University's HHI live exercises, and photomapping volunteer work while at IBM and Amazon/Blink.

These experiences brought attention to local knowledge that was otherwise difficult to see or use, and to the dependence of many information systems on institutions and intermediaries.

My **Globe UI** concepts developed as a medium of exchange and relevant interaction across languages, cultures, and literacy levels. Pictorial representations of surroundings could connect information with a person's understanding of place. Similarly, emphasizing the data itself necessarily included voice, text, SMS, screen readers, and other accessible pathways as integral to the design.

## 2010s 2020s: Cryptography, Web3, and interchain evaluation

I spent years following advances in cryptography, blockchain systems, **Web3**, and technologies for connecting or coordinating multiple chains. I evaluated them against the requirements of individual ownership and continuity, contextual disclosure, voluntary agreements, reciprocal evidence, local-first use, and cooperation under personal authority.

None of the external chain or interchain architectures I examined met the requirements as a whole. Independently identifiable objects, condition-governed transactions, append-only provenance, and verifiable agreement informed the design. Some came close, like Holochain, yet suffered from centralizing architecture that is anathema to sovereignty. Has-Needs does borrow concepts like  eUTXO + NFT pairing as architectural concepts within its own receipt histories and sovereign interaction model.

## 2022 onward: AI-assisted research and architectural development

Beginning in 2022, and after I left Amazon/Blink, generative AI became my main research and design instrument. It helped me examine blockchain and interchain technologies, understand security mechanisms and operational requirements, compare alternatives, and explore different ways to achieve the protections the architecture needed. I also began 'model busting' professionally, evaluating AI tools and creating rubrics for their safe use.

The process was iterative: describing protocols, linking fundamental concepts, exposing constraints, explaining workflows, testing ideas, identifying commonalities, and reducing unnecessary complexity. No other work partner possessed the comprehensive knowledge set that AI tools could provide.

Conversations with Perplexity helped crystallize the semantic triplet **[entity, relation, context]** which brought different human interactions into a common grammar while allowing people to begin with their own dialect and local understanding. Now, my ontology could grow outward through human contact, agreed meanings, and completed exchanges.

The continuing question was:

**What is the smallest set of mechanisms that preserves individual sovereignty, verifiable cooperation, and the ability to accommodate the full range of human interactions?**

As a crisis mapper with the SBTF, I had a steady stream of natural and political disasters to compare Has-Needs to. I could refine the design or promote features that would have been useful in every type of event whether natural or man-made. Has-Needs is the result of rigorous real-world refinement.

My influences included **Buckminster Fuller's systems thinking** and **DSRP**—Distinctions, Systems, Relationships, Perspectives, from Derek Cabrera. I must also credit David Graeber and David Wengrow's *The Dawn of Everything* that confirmed so many of my own reflections on the human species' social diversity, settlement, and governance patterns.

## Knowledge exchange and personal continuity

As a self-controlled personal data environment, Has-needs gave rise to a self-directed economic model growing organically from consent and access rights. The economic vision gives people and communities authority over the knowledge they produce and the terms of its use. Overlays Capture Architecture (OCA) was among the first technological solutions that seemed purpose-built for what I hoped to form, and the discovery of DXOS gave me real hope that Has-Needs could be a technical reality.

As an illustration, consider the sovereign farmer who is able to provide a stream of useful information to neighbors and wider to a regional or national weather authority, from merely sticking an old mobile phone to a post. With OCA, local observations could be pooled and offered to agencies and researchers in exchange for money or benefits and services. Participation could be passive while authorization remained sovereign. Payment and markets could coexist with mutual aid and other forms of exchange in the same platform whose measure of relevance begins with you, here, now. This is one example of the power inherent in sovereignty-based systems.

Personal continuity also affords owner-held provenance and consent for all of one's outputs including writings, creative work, voice, images, and other personal artifacts, and their use in AI-derived representations. Has-Needs is a mechanism for enforcing sovereign control over self, motivation, and derivative products regardless of their origin outside of the self. By engaging in Has-Needs, every person is able to assert their consent through cryptographically secured contracts describing conditions of use. In this paradigm, any personal information discovered without a valid governing contract can be considered unauthorized, and therefore actionable in any jurisdiction, backed by a world-class evidentiary substrate.

The simple-ish mechanism Has-needs represents turns out to be truly remarkable the deeper you look. I feel privileged to be the recipient of this vision and focus, and I am extremely proud of the emerging result.

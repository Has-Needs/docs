# Has-Needs: Origins and Intellectual History
*Founder-reported recollections and design provenance, 20 October 2026. This is a working narrative, not a normative V1 specification or independent verification.*

## Dedication to Ishi
The founder previously dedicated Has-Needs to Ishi. The original dedication wording remains to be recovered rather than invented.

Entering foster care in seventh grade, the founder attended an advanced English class where a teacher brought river stones from the region associated with Ishi and invited students to choose one while reading his story. The founder, already connected deeply to nature as a source of refuge, hope, and strength, felt an affinity with the story of displacement and isolation. The stone was later lost, like many treasured belongings during childhood moves. This personal connection should not be read as equating two distinct histories.

## Childhood movement, memory, and continuity
The founder reports moving more than fifty times before age twenty, including stays in a castle in Italy, a cottage in Grasse, and a château in Antibes, followed by hardship in California, including food scavenging and foster care. Each relocation revealed a different world of relationships, dangers, opportunities, and local knowledge. The founder recalls that decisions might have been different if more of the surrounding situation had been visible at the time. Trauma-related gaps in memory made preservation of personally meaningful history especially important.

The resulting design concern is not that everyone should remain rooted. It is that the **self-quantified sovereign individual** should retain continuity of identity, meaningful relationships, contributions, and evidence of commitments whether rooted or mobile. Personal chains and reciprocal receipts serve aspects of that continuity; the V1 protocol should not be described as already storing arbitrary childhood artifacts.

## Agency, trauma, and system-of-systems thinking
The founder describes early exposure to Buckminster Fuller, peer counseling since 1985, emergency and mass-casualty response as a paramedic, post-trauma debriefing, and participation as a subject in a UCSF stress/trauma study. These experiences collectively informed the conviction that early meaningful agency is essential to recovery and resilience. No single study or episode should be cast as the sole origin of Has-Needs, nor should the founder's clinical hypothesis be presented as universally proven.

**Sovereign Survivor +1** means beginning with the survivor as a sovereign actor and enabling connection with at least one other sovereign participant. Because disaster can disrupt unpredictable parts of any hierarchy, the architecture seeks to empower every surviving endpoint to declare what matters, initiate voluntary exchange, and build coordination that responders can subsequently join. The person affected is informed first, rather than being treated solely as an information source for institutions.

## Global connection, commercial enclosure, and the rediscovery of locality
Early membership in Nomads International, international bulletin board systems, and 2600-oriented network culture brought excitement at the collapse of distance. Commercial platforms subsequently enclosed and partitioned social groups, challenging the open interpersonal promise of networking.

The founder's realization was that the most important digital transformation could be **seeing the world immediately around oneself differently**. In disaster, when possessions and institutions may disappear, a person still occupies physical space. The founding questions became: *Who does my location matter to? When? Where do interests intersect, and how can those intersections be supported?* Geographic relevance need not entail revealing precise whereabouts.

## The Globe UI as a medium of exchange
The Globe UI is intended as a **literacy- and culturally agnostic medium of exchange and relevant interaction**, not merely a map or dashboard. Pictorial representations of surroundings can anchor relevant information to a person's mental self-representation of place, sometimes bypassing dependence on words altogether. Predictive information can reveal impending hardship or opportunity, and sovereign action can turn awareness into prevention or benefit. Successful outcomes may reinforce perceived self-efficacy; this is a designed research hypothesis, not a clinical outcome already established.

Equivalence across speech, text, SMS, screen reader and other accessible pathways remains essential. A pictorial medium should be tested across cultures rather than assumed universally understood.

## Learning from lived communities and research
The founder reports formative observations of Map Kibera and WaveFax and experience with participatory action research, experience sampling, citizen science, SBTF crisis mapping, Uchaguzi, UN-HHI exercises, and photomapping work at Amazon and IBM. These examples showed the potential for previously obscured local information to support self-understanding, cooperation, and meaningful action, as well as the fragility of systems dependent on institutions or intermediaries. They are design influences, not a claim that the architecture is simply a bundle of predecessor features.

## Passive contribution and post-capitalist knowledge exchange
A farmer can mount an old phone on a stick to observe field conditions. Authorized observations can be pooled locally, inform useful predictions, and be offered through voluntary compensated agreements to governance agencies and international researchers. The farmer need not continuously operate the system to contribute. **Participation can be passive while authorization remains active and sovereign.**

This exemplifies the founder's post-capitalist, non-extractive economic vision: people and communities produce knowledge and negotiate its use instead of surrendering it as an unpaid raw material. Payment and markets remain possible; the intended inversion is who holds authority over the relationship and who benefits from its value.

## Further intellectual context
David Graeber and David Wengrow's *The Dawn of Everything* informs the founder's reflections on social diversity and changing patterns of settlement and movement. It does not establish a universal clinical benefit from mobility.

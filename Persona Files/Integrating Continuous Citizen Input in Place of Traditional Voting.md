## Has-Needs and the un-vote

In the Has-Needs system, the steady stream of citizen Needs can serve as a continuous, real-time form of voting on community priorities and initiatives. This approach leverages transparency and active participation to shape governance and resource allocation, making traditional voting less necessary. Here’s how this can be structured:

#### Key Components and Interactions
**Continuous Citizen Input**

**Needs as Citizen Input:** Voluntarily shared Needs may signal community priorities, but do not constitute a census, a compelled vote, or a substitute for legally required elections.
Community Needs: Larger communal Needs, such as infrastructure projects, also impact decision-making.
Transparency and Accountability:

**Private Receipt Evidence:** There is no public ledger of all citizens' Needs. Persona Managers maintain private personal chains. Need discovery is permission-scoped, and accepted completed exchanges leave reciprocal receipts; any civic dashboard uses authorized contributions or aggregates, not global enumeration.
Governance Metrics: Track and display responsiveness and effectiveness of governance bodies based on their ability to meet Needs.
Real-Time Decision-Making:

**Data Visualization:** Use visual tools to display ongoing Needs and their statuses.
Dynamic Prioritization: Automatically prioritize resources and actions based on the frequency and urgency of Needs.

#### Delegation, fulfillment, and civic evidence

A service Need is a smart contract with standard templates and custom options. Its involved Has and Needs remain connected in the WORKING state while the request is delegated through the organization to the worker who fulfills it. That worker becomes preeminent in the recorded outcome, informing the local ontology's self-pruning tree. Future requests can use the recent effective route while retaining the org chart for authorized fallback.

The journal retains accepted responsibility, progress, handoffs, and communications. Resolution encapsulates these in identical receipts written simultaneously to the required participants' chains and mutually verified, including encrypted portions. Agreed no-deal or expiry outcomes are also recorded and can inform personal filters; they are not silently erased or confused with declined candidates.

A Community's composable API can stream communications to secure storage under its chosen responsible parties and existing permissions. Civic views connect who did what with expenditure and outcomes, supporting budget reconciliation and exposing bottlenecks. The choice of standard civic encryption/access rules versus required transparency for publicly funded work remains open, with sensitive location and operational detail protected for safety.

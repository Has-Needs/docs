# Bob and Jim: one exchange, two useful facets

Jim needs a deck repaired using recycled wood. His Need is a smart contract made from a standard service template with custom material, timing, and outcome conditions. Discovery suggests Bob's Has of woodworking capability. Jim chooses the candidate; Bob accepts. Their objects are connected in the WORKING state while the Need executes.

They discuss scope and price, record progress, and agree on the result. The append-only journal retains their communications. If they instead decide not to proceed, that is an outcome of the same contract. An expiry can also be agreed in the template, with release and on-chain recording under those terms. These no-deal records may affect personal filters seeking clean, easy exchanges; ignored candidate suggestions are not contract outcomes.

At resolution, each chooses what to disclose through their facet. Customer name, address, and cost remain encrypted. “Deck,” “recycled wood,” “very happy with the job,” and “accurate quote” can be associated with Bob the woodworker. Jim can choose to show evidence that he pays on time, gives clear directions, and respects a craftsperson's input. They can also keep their facets anonymous to outsiders and accept the resulting interaction limits.

The complete exchange record becomes one canonical receipt, written identically and simultaneously to each participant's personal chain by their communicating Persona Managers. They mutually verify the copies. Even a fully encrypted receipt can be corroborated by matching entries without opening either core chain. Each independently initiates outward random verification hops after completion.

Their authorized exchange evidence contributes to locally relevant ontology and future discovery. There is no additional reputation primitive.

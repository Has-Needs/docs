# Has-Needs
## A social protocol that begins with the individual

**What do you have? What do you need? What can we accomplish together?**

After a flood, a person knows that drinking water is gone, a neighbor needs help, and a usable cart is nearby. Their knowledge, priorities, and capabilities exist before an agency creates an intake record. **Has-Needs begins with that person's ability to express a request, offer help, choose an agreement, and retain evidence of what happened.** Vulnerability does not imply informational poverty.

Has-Needs is a concept and coordination architecture grounded in **system-of-systems thinking**. People, communities, and institutions retain their own purposes and authority while cooperating through a common grammar of exchange. Software gives that grammar an operational form. Its broader purpose is to create conditions in which agency, reciprocal relationships, useful knowledge, and collective capacity reinforce one another.

We begin as sovereign beings. Society is sustained by **exchanges of value**: care, knowledge, time, labor, resources, and commitments. The terms of each exchange belong to the people participating in it.

Yet across humanitarian aid, capitalist finance, governance, and software, institutions commonly control the systems that recognize and record those exchanges. People can contribute enormously without keeping a secure record of what they gave, received, or accomplished.

**Has-Needs preserves reciprocal evidence of value exchange in participant-owned histories.** When an exchange is resolved, an identical receipt is written to each participant's **private receipt chain**. Authorized receipt-linked evidence can be checked without exposing or traversing the underlying private chains. Together, these independent chains form a **forest of chains**, rather than a single ledger controlled by an institution.

That foundation protects sovereignty: people can participate, form relationships, and build on their experience without surrendering their personal data or the record of their exchanges to someone else.

### One small grammar, many human worlds

More than fifteen years of design, disaster experience, and reduction converged on a decisive milestone: the **semantic data triplet, `[entity, relation, context]`**. Three relation states—`HAS`, `NEED`, and `WORKING`—provide a shared way to express exchanges without inventing a different system for every kind of interaction. The options became fewer; the range of possible interactions grew.

**The interface and basic actions are provided; the person's meaning is not prescribed.** Has-Needs supplies a graphical way to give, receive, and exchange through a defined, limited vocabulary of basic contract primitives. People need not build the interface, write text, or select a preexisting institutional category. Each person chooses what they have and need, expressed in locally meaningful ways. Their ontology grows from those voluntary contributions and from interactions with people nearby. More sophisticated users may create custom contract extensions, but these do not automatically become official primitives.

The first layer is **human exchange**. Mutual assistance, sharing knowledge, barter, care, education, and commerce all use the same basic interaction; payment is simply one possible term. The Composable API lets services participate without taking over that foundation. A university's recognized educational exchange can result in a degree, and the university can later attest to the degree from its own records without giving a requester access to the student's private chain or underlying history.

### From the individual outward—and back again

The process begins with two familiar expressions:

**“I need…”** something to obtain, solve, or accomplish.

**“I have…”** something to contribute: knowledge, resources, abilities, or time.

These intentions travel outward through relevant communities. A Need carries its own conditions for an exchange. Matching intelligence, including AI, suggests possibilities; **people decide**. When they accept a match, the Has and Need enter `WORKING` together while the Need executes its authorized conditions. Its append-only **pending journal** preserves the accepted commitment and authorized progress. The Need owner is the primary recipient of updates; a relay or host gains no authority to accept an assignment merely by carrying the message.

When the work is done, the participants independently mark it complete. Each may add comments and separately choose whether their own contributions are plaintext or encrypted. Both review the proposed transaction, communications, and exposure choices; neither may edit the other's comments. Each marks **Ready to commit** on the same version. Their Persona Managers then write identical transaction receipts to private chains; matching valid receipts on all required chains establish recorded completion.

Mutual cancellation and partial fulfillment use the same receipt mechanism to record the agreed outcome. Unresolved commitments remain in pending evidence; silence supplies neither consent nor blame. A matching receipt authenticates the agreed record, rather than independently proving every physical fact.

**The Persona Manager acts as a concierge, managing interactions through any number of user-created personas while their underlying personal chain remains sacrosanct.** Bob the Builder can associate completed deck projects with his professional persona without revealing his social persona or the customer's private identity.

**The network facilitates relationships. It does not own them.**

### Designed emergence: complex outcomes from simple beginnings

Has-Needs began with the **lone individual in the disaster moment**. Before an institution arrives, what do they need, what can they do, and how can their voice, agency, and valuable information be preserved? Its purpose was to **mitigate disaster trauma**. Its creator studied psychology, sociology, community trauma, and Indigenous studies at UMass Boston while testing ideas through crisis mapping, tabletop scenarios, and real disaster work. Theory and practice continually challenged the design.

The design examines desired prosocial endpoints—reciprocity, dignity, cooperation, accountability, resilience, and trauma mitigation—then works back to the smallest primitives that create the conditions for those behaviors to emerge naturally.

This is *designed emergence*. Sovereign people express Has and Need, voluntarily accept commitments, and preserve their value exchanges on Persona-Manager-maintained private chains. From these interactions can emerge communities formed by consent, informed trust, knowledge shared under its holders' authority, and accountability for accepted obligations. These larger functions arise through composition of the same primitives. Each participant keeps local authority while making selected relationships interoperable.

**Designed emergence connects the scale of one exchange to the scale of a community.** A fulfilled request leaves reciprocal evidence; authorized evidence informs later matching and relationship judgment; useful paths make subsequent cooperation easier; participants can combine commitments into ongoing communities and coordinated projects. The design deliberately arranges these feedback relationships. The particular organizations and solutions remain open to the people involved.

At the system-of-systems level, a farmer, transporter, neighborhood group, researcher, and public agency can coordinate without adopting one institution's identity system, vocabulary, or command structure. They connect through agreed conditions and bounded disclosures. Local autonomy and collective action operate together.

The ontology preserves a contextual map of what has worked alongside participant-originated meanings. Successful fulfillment contributes resolution evidence; cancellation and unresolved work remain distinct. This keeps the feedback tied to actual interactions without turning private lives into behavioral profiles.

**Protecting Indigenous wisdom and traditional knowledge is a central benefit.** Its holders and communities determine what may be preserved, taught, shared, or withheld according to their own governance and cultural protocols. Individual participation does not transfer collectively governed knowledge to a platform or another participant.

As exchanges accumulate across the forest of chains, participants and communities can discover useful patterns in the evidence they choose to share. A Need can accommodate mutual aid, barter, monetary payment, or any combination as ordinary exchange terms, without changing the underlying protocol.

The same foundations serve neighbors, farmers, researchers, and public agencies. Capabilities developed in ordinary life remain useful during a crisis.

### The disaster moment: one person, then a community

Imagine one person trapped in a collapsed building. They need rescue. But they may know where others are trapped or which route is safe. **That firsthand data is priceless**—something they *have* to contribute, not a resource automatically claimed by an institution.

A second person arrives. They can help or relay the Need. A third has medical skills; a fourth has tools. From five people to five thousand, the same simple interactions let them express needs, contribute knowledge, make commitments, and organize recovery. **Each person is a seed of a healthier community**, not an entry in someone else's aid database.

Has-Needs begins **local-first**, in each individual's sphere of awareness and capability. Transport adapters carry the same interaction semantics over Bluetooth, peer-to-peer links, SMS, radio, and other available media; nearby people can respond, relay a message, or extend the sphere of possible cooperation. Wider networks and institutions may participate as relationships extend outward. This is the native starting point of the architecture.

The person trapped beneath rubble knows their own condition and surroundings in ways even responders equipped with drones cannot fully discover from outside. Being able to send a message with a credible possibility of being heard offers an opportunity for purposeful action **before anyone replies or rescue begins**. Supporting that agency is a deliberate trauma-informed design objective whose effects require evaluation. People control what private data they share; resolved exchanges leave matching receipts on their own chains.

**From one person to five thousand, sovereignty isn't sacrificed for cooperation. It is where cooperation begins.**


### Reciprocal recognition in a new community

A trapped person offers firsthand situational knowledge and explicitly authorized rescue directions that rescuers need, while responders offer assistance the person needs: both sides contribute essential value. A town can similarly offer a QR entry point to locally available resources and opportunities. Human-curated ontology mappings can help newcomers express skills in terms local residents recognize, from formal trades to everyday practical help and companionship. People can observe possible support before deciding whether to participate. This may foster agency and cooperative recognition across unfamiliar social boundaries; any effects on trauma, belonging, or xenophobia remain research questions. Assistance is never contingent on a recipient's ability to contribute.

### Personal information and portable evidence

Has-Needs starts with the individual's authority over personal information. A person authorizes a specific use through a bounded exchange contract or permission token: who may use which information, for what purpose, and for how long. Institutions may contribute their own signed attestations without taking ownership of the person's identity or private receipt chain. Corresponding personal-chain receipts preserve evidence of work, learning, cooperation, and other completed exchanges that can be cryptographically checked wherever the person goes, including when home-country institutions are inaccessible. Technical verification is independent of a jurisdiction's institutional sophistication; legal admissibility and recognition remain matters for the receiving jurisdiction.

Revocable live access lets a person terminate future access through the controlled interface. Previously disclosed copies remain outside that technical power; legal recognition of authorization and remedies depends on the applicable law and agreement.

### Geographic possibility, discovery, and human authority

Geography describes **where fulfillment is possible**: a service area, route, destination, or remote capability. Access to a truck can expand pickup options without exposing the owner's whereabouts. Actual participant position is not retrievable through protocol operations or stored in shared objects, journals, receipts, or routing headers. A separately authorized rendezvous instruction carries its own disclosure risk.

Discovery begins in authorized local communities and extends outward as permitted. There is no protocol-wide inventory of everyone's private Hases and Needs. An agency can request reports or aggregate views; participants authorize the information that makes that picture possible.

Trust comes from human judgment of authorized reciprocal event evidence and relationship paths. Has-Needs stores no numerical reputation score. Its grey-list flags demonstrated receipt-hash discrepancies, separately from unresolved work or limited history.

AI translates, interprets, and proposes candidates within its assigned information scope. People authorize commitments and disclosures; a separate protocol layer enforces those permissions, including narrowly defined prior authorizations. AI components hold no authority to commit contractual transitions. The common grammar also works without AI.

The primary rich interface is a globe or map of authorized possibilities. Text, speech, SMS, terminal, and screen-reader interfaces preserve the same meaning and authority boundaries.

### Communities and shared fulfillment

An ongoing community is an open-ended Need for coordination within which individual exchanges resolve. Membership alone grants no authority over members' resources. A pooled Need can grant bounded authority through explicitly accepted terms; a consolidated shipment can instead preserve each item's individual authorization. Communities and sub-communities combine commitments while participants retain their own identities and histories.

### Help on people's timelines

Aid commonly arrives in shipments, budget cycles, and scheduled distributions, requiring people to adapt to whatever is available that day. Has-Needs reverses the starting point: individuals express Needs when they arise, and independent Working relationships advance whenever relevant capabilities become available. Housing, transport, livelihoods, care, and community rebuilding can progress in parallel without synchronizing every participant to one delivery window. This is the same mechanism that supports everyday services and cooperation, not a separate disaster workflow. A resource must still exist to be matched; the design preserves actionable demand and enables ongoing coordination.

### Research and institutions as participants

Communities can originate questions as Needs; researchers can offer methods and publish standing participation Needs. Participants choose what to share and can co-design studies that address local priorities. The same owner-controlled composable API lets banks, educators, aid agencies, and other competent issuers attest bounded facts without owning the participant's wider identity. Open-ended value permits monetary and nonmonetary exchange; replaceable cryptographic techniques provide a path to post-quantum migration. These are design capabilities to deploy and investigate, not promises that every intended outcome has already been measured.

### Sovereign commerce and professional provenance

The same HAS/NEED/WORKING grammar enables commerce without a fixed catalog of what counts as value. A sovereign participant can offer goods, skill, time, expertise, an attested social-media audience threshold, or intangible contributions on chosen terms. A business can publish a **standing Need** describing whom or what it seeks; eligible participants' Persona Managers evaluate it privately and offer the opportunity to their owners. **The business sees only responses that participants authorize**, not a list of everyone who matched. As new people join or become eligible, the Need can continue to create opportunities.

Creators and tradespeople can expose a limited **professional facet**: verified training or apprenticeship, selected reciprocal exchange receipts, repeat engagements, project types, and cadence of work. A customer may search these authorized patterns to reach their own comfort threshold without relying on a universal reputation score. A return visit may mean maintenance, a new phase, a repeat satisfied client, or rework; a receipt authenticates an agreed record, not independent quality or completeness. The worker chooses what to disclose, and counterparties' confidentiality remains protected. These capabilities emerge from sovereign Hases and Needs, Persona Manager facets, voluntary WORKING commitments, Need-owned journals, and independently held reciprocal receipts—not a central customer database or public personal ledger.

### Institutional value and research

Direct expression makes requests actionable without first reconstructing people's intentions from observed behavior. Accepted commitments, authorized progress, and resolutions provide a basis for identifying delays, duplicate effort, and unmet demand. Agencies can participate in the same exchange grammar as individuals, with accountability grounded in the obligations they accept.

The white paper examines how these mechanisms affect agency, fulfillment time, disclosure, cooperation, resilience, and operational cost. Its county funding proposal is a **one-time fee of 5% of mutually assessed, realized efficiency savings**, after deployment and operating costs—not a share of total disaster damage. Measurement distinguishes comparable historical costs from needs and work made newly visible by participation.

The architecture states what participants can express, authorize, coordinate, and preserve. The research agenda tests the resulting human and economic effects. Its central proposition is that **sovereign local action can generate coherent collective capacity without transferring authority over people to a central platform**.

### Comparative architectural distinction

**Comparative investigation to date, assisted by leading commercially available AI models, has identified no other project that operates in the same way as Has-Needs: combining this breadth of coordination with the economy of its primitives and participant sovereignty throughout.**

The architectural achievement is what these primitives accomplish together. The same Has/Need/WORKING grammar carries participant-defined meaning into voluntary commitments, preserves reciprocal evidence, and supports communities, commerce, research, governance, disaster response, and recovery. These functions remain connected expressions of one architecture as cooperation grows across independently governed people and organizations.

**Designed emergence makes that economy consequential.** Successful exchanges supply contextual evidence for subsequent discovery and relationship judgment; participants compose commitments into collective action while retaining their authority. Has-Needs brings these mechanisms together so that useful coordination at one scale contributes to capacity at another. This combination—and the breadth achieved without multiplying the foundational primitives—is the basis of its distinctive architectural efficacy.

# Has-Needs
## A social protocol that begins with the individual

**What do you have? What do you need? What can we accomplish together?**

We begin as sovereign beings. Society is sustained by **exchanges of value**: care, knowledge, time, labor, resources, and commitments. Much of that value is never expressed in money. It is what people contribute to one another and accomplish together.

Yet across humanitarian aid, capitalist finance, governance, and software, institutions commonly control the systems that recognize and record those exchanges. People can contribute enormously without keeping a secure record of what they gave, received, or accomplished.

**Has-Needs starts by preserving value exchange on a permanent, immutable substrate owned by the participants themselves.** When an exchange is resolved, an identical receipt is written to each participant's **private receipt chain**. Their chains can interact with others in the network to verify matching receipts without exposing unrelated private data. Together, these independent chains form a **forest of chains**, rather than a single ledger controlled by an institution.

That foundation protects sovereignty: people can participate, form relationships, and build on their experience without surrendering their personal data or the record of their exchanges to someone else.

### From the individual outward—and back again

The process begins with two familiar expressions:

**“I need…”** something to obtain, solve, or accomplish.

**“I have…”** something to contribute: knowledge, resources, abilities, or time.

These intentions travel outward through relevant communities. Matching intelligence, including AI, can suggest possibilities; **people decide** whether to proceed and on what terms. When they agree, their exchange **enters a working state**. The accepted commitment remains distinct from a completed outcome until the interaction is resolved.

Only then are the corresponding receipts written to the participants' private chains. The receipts can be checked against one another while unrelated private data stays under the participants' control.

**The network facilitates relationships. It does not own them.**

### Designed emergence: complex outcomes from simple beginnings

Has-Needs began with the **lone individual in the disaster moment**. Before an institution arrives, what do they need, what can they do, and how can their voice, agency, and valuable information be preserved? Its purpose was to **mitigate disaster trauma**. Its creator studied psychology, sociology, community trauma, and Indigenous studies at UMass Boston while testing ideas through crisis mapping, tabletop scenarios, and real disaster work. Theory and practice continually challenged the design.

Instead of starting with software and adding features, the work proceeded **backward from desired human behaviors**—reciprocity, dignity, cooperation, accountability, and resilience—to the smallest mechanisms that could support them.

This is *designed emergence*. Sovereign people express Has and Need, voluntarily accept commitments, and permanently record their value exchanges on their own private chains. From these same interactions can arise communities formed by consent, informed trust, practical knowledge from completed work, and accountability for accepted obligations. They are not separate applications layered onto the protocol.

As exchanges accumulate across the forest of chains, communities can develop a better understanding of **how value circulates, where it builds capacity, and where it leaves**—without converting every contribution into money or handing that data to a central owner.

The same foundations serve neighbors, farmers, researchers, and public agencies. Capabilities developed in ordinary life remain useful during a crisis.

### The disaster moment: one person, then a community

Imagine one person trapped in a collapsed building. They need rescue. But they may know where others are trapped or which route is safe. **That firsthand data is priceless**—something they *have* to contribute, not a resource automatically claimed by an institution.

A second person arrives. They can help or relay the Need. A third has medical skills; a fourth has tools. From five people to five thousand, the same simple interactions let them express needs, contribute knowledge, make commitments, and organize recovery. **Each person is a seed of a healthier community**, not an entry in someone else's aid database.

Has-Needs was designed **local-first** for this moment. Where devices and connections permit, messages can travel directly through Bluetooth and peer-to-peer mesh relays without depending on the internet or a centralized service. They can reach wider networks when connections return. People control what private data they share; completed value exchanges leave matching receipts on their own chains.

**From one person to five thousand, sovereignty isn't sacrificed for cooperation. It is where cooperation begins.**

# Has-Needs
## A social protocol that begins with the individual

**What do you have? What do you need? What can we accomplish together?**

We begin as sovereign beings. Society is sustained by **exchanges of value**: care, knowledge, time, labor, resources, and commitments. The terms of each exchange belong to the people participating in it.

Yet across humanitarian aid, capitalist finance, governance, and software, institutions commonly control the systems that recognize and record those exchanges. People can contribute enormously without keeping a secure record of what they gave, received, or accomplished.

**Has-Needs starts by preserving value exchange on a permanent, immutable substrate owned by the participants themselves.** When an exchange is resolved, an identical receipt is written to each participant's **private receipt chain**. Their chains can interact with others in the network to verify matching receipts without exposing unrelated private data. Together, these independent chains form a **forest of chains**, rather than a single ledger controlled by an institution.

That foundation protects sovereignty: people can participate, form relationships, and build on their experience without surrendering their personal data or the record of their exchanges to someone else.

### One small grammar, many human worlds

More than fifteen years of design, disaster experience, and reduction converged on a decisive milestone: the **semantic data triplet, `[entity, relation, context]`**. Three relation states—`HAS`, `NEED`, and `WORKING`—provide a shared way to express exchanges without inventing a different system for every kind of interaction. The options became fewer; the range of possible interactions grew.

**The interface and basic actions are provided; the person's meaning is not prescribed.** Has-Needs supplies a graphical way to give, receive, and exchange through a defined, limited vocabulary of basic contract primitives. People need not build the interface, write text, or select a preexisting institutional category. Each person chooses what they have and need, expressed in locally meaningful ways. Their ontology grows from those voluntary contributions and from interactions with people nearby. More sophisticated users may create custom contract extensions, but these do not automatically become official primitives.

The first layer is **human exchange**. Mutual assistance, sharing knowledge, barter, care, education, and commerce all use the same basic interaction; payment is simply one possible term. The Composable API lets services participate without taking over that foundation. A university's recognized educational exchange can result in a degree, and the university can later attest to the degree from its own records without giving a requester access to the student's private chain or underlying history.

### From the individual outward—and back again

The process begins with two familiar expressions:

**“I need…”** something to obtain, solve, or accomplish.

**“I have…”** something to contribute: knowledge, resources, abilities, or time.

These intentions travel outward through relevant communities. A Need carries its own conditions for an exchange. Matching intelligence, including AI, suggests possibilities; **people decide**. When they accept a match, the Has and Need enter `WORKING` together while the Need executes.

When the work is done, the participants independently mark it complete. Each may add comments and separately choose whether their own contributions are plaintext or encrypted. Both review the proposed transaction, communications, and exposure choices; neither may edit the other's comments. Each marks **Ready to commit** on the same version. Their Persona Managers then write identical transaction receipts to private chains; matching hashes establish completion.

**The Persona Manager acts as a concierge, managing interactions through any number of user-created personas while their underlying personal chain remains sacrosanct.** Bob the Builder can associate completed deck projects with his professional persona without revealing his social persona or the customer's private identity.

**The network facilitates relationships. It does not own them.**

### Designed emergence: complex outcomes from simple beginnings

Has-Needs began with the **lone individual in the disaster moment**. Before an institution arrives, what do they need, what can they do, and how can their voice, agency, and valuable information be preserved? Its purpose was to **mitigate disaster trauma**. Its creator studied psychology, sociology, community trauma, and Indigenous studies at UMass Boston while testing ideas through crisis mapping, tabletop scenarios, and real disaster work. Theory and practice continually challenged the design.

The design examines desired prosocial endpoints—reciprocity, dignity, cooperation, accountability, resilience, and trauma mitigation—then works back to the smallest primitives that create the conditions for those behaviors to emerge naturally.

This is *designed emergence*. Sovereign people express Has and Need, voluntarily accept commitments, and preserve their value exchanges on Persona-Manager-maintained private chains. From these interactions can emerge communities formed by consent, informed trust, knowledge shared under its holders' authority, and accountability for accepted obligations. These are consequences of the same primitives, not separate applications layered onto the protocol.

**Protecting Indigenous wisdom and traditional knowledge is a central benefit.** Its holders and communities determine what may be preserved, taught, shared, or withheld according to their own governance and cultural protocols. Individual participation does not transfer collectively governed knowledge to a platform or another participant.

As exchanges accumulate across the forest of chains, participants and communities can discover useful patterns in the evidence they choose to share. A Need can accommodate mutual aid, barter, monetary payment, or any combination as ordinary exchange terms, without changing the underlying protocol.

The same foundations serve neighbors, farmers, researchers, and public agencies. Capabilities developed in ordinary life remain useful during a crisis.

### The disaster moment: one person, then a community

Imagine one person trapped in a collapsed building. They need rescue. But they may know where others are trapped or which route is safe. **That firsthand data is priceless**—something they *have* to contribute, not a resource automatically claimed by an institution.

A second person arrives. They can help or relay the Need. A third has medical skills; a fourth has tools. From five people to five thousand, the same simple interactions let them express needs, contribute knowledge, make commitments, and organize recovery. **Each person is a seed of a healthier community**, not an entry in someone else's aid database.

Has-Needs begins **local-first**, in each individual's sphere of awareness and capability. Participating devices communicate through Bluetooth and peer-to-peer mesh connections within range; nearby people can respond, relay a message, or extend the sphere of possible cooperation. Wider networks and institutions may participate as relationships extend outward. This is the native starting point of the architecture.

The person trapped beneath rubble knows their own condition and surroundings in ways even responders equipped with drones cannot fully discover from outside. Being able to send a message with a credible possibility of being heard offers an opportunity for purposeful action **before anyone replies or rescue begins**. Supporting that agency is a deliberate trauma-informed design objective whose effects require evaluation. People control what private data they share; resolved exchanges leave matching receipts on their own chains.

**From one person to five thousand, sovereignty isn't sacrificed for cooperation. It is where cooperation begins.**

### Sovereign commerce and professional provenance

The same HAS/NEED/WORKING grammar enables commerce without a fixed catalog of what counts as value. A sovereign participant can offer goods, skill, time, expertise, an attested social-media audience threshold, or intangible contributions on chosen terms. A business can publish a **standing Need** describing whom or what it seeks; eligible participants' Persona Managers evaluate it privately and offer the opportunity to their owners. **The business sees only responses that participants authorize**, not a list of everyone who matched. As new people join or become eligible, the Need can continue to create opportunities.

Creators and tradespeople can expose a limited **professional facet**: verified training or apprenticeship, selected reciprocal exchange receipts, repeat engagements, project types, and cadence of work. A customer may search these authorized patterns to reach their own comfort threshold without relying on a universal reputation score. A return visit may mean maintenance, a new phase, a repeat satisfied client, or rework; a receipt authenticates an agreed record, not independent quality or completeness. The worker chooses what to disclose, and counterparties' confidentiality remains protected. These capabilities emerge from sovereign Hases and Needs, Persona Manager facets, voluntary WORKING commitments, Need-owned journals, and independently held reciprocal receipts—not a central customer database or public personal ledger.

# Has-Needs

Has-Needs begins with four elements: 
- **sovereign individual** who decides what they offer, what they seek, and how they participate.
- **Has**, something available: knowledge, skills, resources, time, or an opportunity.
- **Need**, something to obtain, accomplish, learn, repair, or change.
- **Community**, sovereign participants gathered around any shared purpose.

These four elements support cooperation from an exchange between neighbors to coordination among communities and institutions.

People already exchange value in everyday life. Someone offers a ride, shares a harvest, teaches a skill, or helps repair a home. The value may involve payment, barter, care, knowledge, or mutual aid; the people involved determine what matters. Has-Needs gives these relationships a common way to begin, develop, and build evidence of what has been accomplished.

### From discovery to agreement

As independent objects, Need and Has circulate to find compatible match-candidates. People express both in their own terms, with context that helps the system understand them. Match discovery in the background brings relevant possibilities into view.

**From Matching to human judgment.** An AI discovered match-candidate is an invitation to consider a relationship. The Need owner selects which fits best, then the Has owner is given the choice to complete this pairing. Their mutual consent moves the pairing into the **WORKING** state, where details of the exchange are discussed and the two objects are removed from general matching activity.

While in the WORKING state, an append-only journal holds the developing agreement and progress as the parties work toward completion. Consummation occurs when the participants fulfill and mutually acknowledge the agreed exchange.

### Reciprocal receipts, a forest of chains

When all involved parties accept that the exchange is complete, they select which aspects of the exchange to make public and which to encrypt, then everything accrued while WORKING is bundled into a single receipt and written identically to the chain of each participant. They mutually verify that the copies match, and then continue the chain hopping validation behavior.

Each personal chain preserves its owner's exchange history. Reciprocal receipts connect these independent histories into a **forest of chains** with linked mutual attestations for every event. As a whole, the system creates networks of shared experiences that inform the matching ontology so it can expand and grow while always being locally relevant.

### Jitterbug and the network's ongoing activity

**"Jitterbug" is an adaptive, decentralized network topology:** inspired by R. Buckminster Fuller's geometric discoveries, this topology maintains a low energy base-state that can expand and adapt to blockages without need for central overarching control layer. Each node only needs to know its state and that of its immediate neighbor to support adaptive, self-healing message transport.

This architecture brings together three kinds of activity:

- **Background discovery and chain-hopping.** Has/Need discovery continually finds match-candidates, while randomized hops corroborate records across the forest. These ubiquitous processes run in the background, independently of a particular negotiation.
- **Direct peer-to-peer WORKING.** The Has owner and Need owner communicate, negotiate, carry out their exchange, and write and verify their reciprocal receipts. Similarly, any updates to the status of a resource or efforts to fulfill the Need are written to the Need first, thus ensuring every **affected entity** maintains the best situational awareness that the system can provide.
- **Distributed ontology sharing and comparison.** An individual ontology can be shared in whole or part, with neighbors. It is built up from completed exchanges and participant inputs to reflect local activity.

An ontology is a way of describing what things mean and how they relate. Here, it grows from people's language and the exchanges that actually fulfill their needs. Communities can compare these understandings while keeping localized meanings and context. Shared learning helps future discovery without requiring a single central vocabulary, and makes the entire system increasingly relevant over time by capturing dialects and cultural nuance.

### Continuity and accountability

Because reciprocal receipts are written together and mutually checked, they should always agree. Background corroboration can reveal a later discrepancy and invoke **grey listing** of the involved chains. The grey list identifies an anomaly for attention; personal filters may exclude any grey listed chain from participation, but Has-Needs recognizes that trust is malleable in crisis and that redemption is required in human affairs, so no permanent ban or exclusion occurs.

Being 'grey listed' may limit discovery or participation, or prompt a human conversation about the circumstances. Even an innocent discrepancy can make further cooperation less comfortable. This gives the parties a practical incentive to reconcile their records together on-chain, preserving evidence of both the discrepancy and its resolution.

If an anomaly remains unresolved, verifiable continuity stops at that point, but subsequent exchanges can still be verified and valid. They form a **short verifiable chain**, measured from the anomaly rather than through the whole history. A short chain can also simply reflect a new participant with few completed exchanges. People can assess the available evidence in the context of the relationship they are considering.

### Sovereignty at every scale

A foundational tenet of Has-Needs was absolute individual sovereignty. This requires not exposing one's core chain or physical location under any circumstances. So, the **Persona Manager** acts as an intermediary, providing user-created 'facets' through which all activity can take place safely. This is extremely convenient to manage interactions in life as a tradesperson, a contractor, and a parent - all aspects of one's "self" that interact with distinct parts of their surroundings. The Persona Manager also affords anonymity without exposing personal details. As messages transit, each Persona Manager is able to act on relevant ones without broadcasting intent or capabilities of its owner.

Communities use the same relationships recursively. A group can express collecctive Has and Needs, work with another group, and join broader communities while its members retain their own authority. **Self, Other, and N Others** describe the same pattern at increasing scales. This economy of elements lets cooperation grow through the relationships people choose without extra layers of architecture and governing rulesets.

Participation begins locally and extends through available peer-to-peer links, messaging, and other accessible transports. The envisioned Globe UI offers an entirely personal geographic view of possibilities, alongside text, speech, SMS, terminal, and assistive interfaces. AI can help interpret language and suggest match-candidates, then people choose final matches and ultimately whether to participate, commit, and disclose.

The same records and lightweight protocol that helps neighbors coordinate can make institutional commitments and outcomes accountable to the people concerned. A community can organize a delivery, a responder can coordinate assistance, and a public agency can record fulfillment of an accepted request while respecting individual sovereignty.

### Roots in Mapping, Indigenous Sovereignty, and Community Knowledge

Has-Needs grew from a lifetime of trauma exposure and a long engagement with participatory mapping, Indigenous self-determination, community resilience, and disaster response. These experiences revealed a common principle: **people are the primary authorities on their own lives, their communities, and the meaning of their knowledge.**

Mapping offered an especially powerful insight. A map can do far more than describe where things are. In the hands of a community, it can express local knowledge, reveal relationships, identify possibilities, and support collective action. Indigenous approaches to knowledge and sovereignty reinforced the importance of respecting who holds data, who may share it, and the conditions under which it may be used.

Through community mapping initiatives and international disaster response, these principles converged into a larger question: What if people could do more than contribute information to someone else's map? What if they could express their own needs and capabilities, discover one another, form agreements, and preserve the value of their cooperation?

Has-Needs extends that thinking beyond geography into a shared architecture for human relationships. **The map becomes a doorway to possibility; sovereignty becomes the foundation of participation; and cooperation becomes something people can initiate, shape, and carry forward themselves.** After 15 years of steady effort and research, the technological foundation has finally been achieved through recent advances.
